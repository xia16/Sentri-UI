# The owner's eye

Rules say what a screen may not do. This page says what the owner sees as good, so a builder can aim at it and a judge can stand in for it. It is drawn from the owner's rulings (`calibration/answers.json`, the rejected examples in `briefs/gate.md`) and the approved screens (Farrowing, Inspection). Every builder reads it before the critique, and every judge reads it before calibrating. Add to it whenever the owner rules on a pair.

## The house references

The owner points to four live screens as the product's taste: Home · Today, Unit, More actions and the Farrowing room, rendered in [references/](references/README.md) with what each teaches. They are the first thing to look at and the last thing to compare against. Base designs on what the product already does well, not on outside apps.

## What good looks like here

The approved screens (Farrowing room, Task overview) share one quiet language:

- **One hero per group.** A single value carries the group: a large mono figure (the reading, the live count, "8 / 19"). It is big because everything around it is small. It is not bold-heavy and it doesn't sit in a coloured block.
- **Small, quiet support.** Labels, units, timestamps and "who/when" sit small and grey under or beside the figure they qualify. The contrast in size between the hero and its support is what makes the hierarchy.
- **Light structure.** A pale canvas, white cards with a thin line and a soft radius, one card per topic. Section headings sit outside the cards, small and regular weight, sometimes with a line icon. Rows inside a card are split by hairlines, not by boxes.
- **Weight is rare.** Regular weight is the default. Bold is for the hero figure and, at most, one row title. One dark filled control per screen at most: the action.
- **Air.** Generous padding inside cards and steady gaps between them. Nothing is crammed, and nothing floats in empty space either.

## What the owner rejected, and why

| Pair | What changed | Owner | The lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| c1 | Same layout, every text and number made bigger to meet the floors | "better now, but a lot more cluttered; I would not call this passing" | **Enlarging everything destroys hierarchy.** When the support grows towards the hero, nothing leads and the screen feels full. Raise only what is acted on; keep quiet text quiet (11px is allowed for it). |
| c2 | Cards removed, facts laid straight on the canvas, sections run together | "calmer but no visual hierarchy; just a bunch of information put on a screen, no UI" | **Removing structure is not simplifying.** The cards and section headings are what make it a designed screen. Calm comes from fewer facts and quieter support, not from taking the containers away. |
| c3 | Metric tiles with bold values, bold section titles, every section in its own heavy box, bigger controls | "the left one is still better; yours looks like a bulky brute" | **More boxes and more bold read as heavy, not organised.** The original's one big light reading in one card beat three bold tiles plus more cards. Organising a screen by adding containers and weight is the most common wrong turn, and judges fall for it too. |

## How to use it

- **Builders**: in the critique (`briefs/build.md` §1a) and every self-crit (§1d), hold your direction against these three lessons by name. A direction that adds boxes, bold or size to "organise" the screen is c3 until proven otherwise.
- **Judges**: before calibrating, read this page. Then judge as the owner would: prefer the lighter screen with one clear hero over the one with more structure, more weight or bigger type, unless the heavier one fixes a real problem the lighter one can't.
- A polish pass should look like the same screen, improved. If someone who knows the original wouldn't recognise it, it wasn't a polish.

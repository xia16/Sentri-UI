The idea

The record book, carried into the barn

A farm runs on promises: this sow will be checked on this day, this litter counted before this hour. Field Ledger treats every promise as a line in a book — scheduled, done, signed, and if late, said so.

Farm software usually pretends work happens on time. It doesn't. The pregnancy re-check slips two days; the farrowing entry lands at 23:00. This direction's whole personality comes from refusing to hide that: the delta row — scheduled date struck through, actual date beside it, the difference printed as a colored chip — is the signature gesture, and the registry look (warm paper, ink text, hairline ledger rules, deep olive as the working color) exists to make that honesty feel calm instead of accusatory. It is the trustworthy evolution of Sentri's shipped system, not a rebrand from zero.

Everything on the record.

Every action becomes a dated, signed line: "200 of 200 checked · G. Hansen · 23:00." No vapor states. If it isn't written, it didn't happen; if it is written, it is never rewritten.

The schedule is a promise.

Scheduled and actual always appear together. Lateness is a first-class fact with its own typography — never a shame state, never hidden in a detail view.

Calm paper, loud status.

One warm ground, ink text, editorial hairlines. Color appears only when it means one of five things. Amber is load-bearing — reserved for "due now," never decorative.

The mark

The sign-off · rule + check + full stop
Primary lockup
Sentri.

Two strokes from the ledger itself. The rule is the ruled line of the book — the row where the entry belongs. The check is the auditor's tick: it plants below the rule and strikes up past the margin, the physical gesture of signing a row off. Ink does the ruling; olive does the deciding — the same division of labor the product uses, where the ground stays quiet and the brand color only ever marks an action.

The wordmark is Fraunces semibold, and it always ends in an olive full stop — the record closes every line it opens. That period is the mark's smallest ambassador: it reappears at the end of confirmations in-product ("Written to the room record.").

Construction: the symbol sits on a 96×72 grid; rule weight 9, check weight 10, both square-capped like a nib. The check's apex hits the cap height of the wordmark. Clearspace = one rule-length ÷ 4. Below 24px the rule drops and the check thickens — the small cut is a redraw, never a shrink.

Typography

Fraunces · Plus Jakarta Sans · IBM Plex Mono

Two of three faces are kept from the shipped Sentri system — Plus Jakarta Sans and IBM Plex Mono already live on the farm's phones, so migration costs nothing and muscle memory survives. Status must survive sunlight.

Every status ships as a pair: a fill tone for dots and bars, and a darker text tone that clears 4.5:1 on record white — checked against the worst screen on the farm, at noon, through a wet glove. Two laws on top: never color alone (every status prints its word — "Due now", "+2d late" — beside its hue), and the brand may not moonlight: olive marks actions and IDs, and if a surface is olive, it is a button.

Writes 100 records · signed G. Hansen

Two hundred sows, one ruled book.

The density answer is the ledger table itself: pen groups are the only containers, rows are 56px with hairline separators, and each row is a fixed three-column grid — mono ID, status line with its delta, one 48px action. The pattern repeats without fatigue because nothing in it decorates.

1Pen as chapter. Recessed paper headers (PEN A1 · 26/52) break 200 rows into countable chapters; distant pens collapse to their tallies.
2The delta row in anger. 000325: schedule struck through, actual beside it, "+2d late" chip. Lateness recorded, not scolded — and never hidden.
3Action = urgency. Only rows that need a hand get a filled button — amber for due, red for attention. Done rows demote to a quiet edit glyph.
4The signed CTA. Submit states what it writes and who signs it. The button is the stamp.

Density rules

The grammar of a full page

Row anatomy — 56 over 48.

List rows are 56px tall; the touch zone inside is never under 48px, primary CTAs never under 56px. Three columns, fixed: 66px mono ID · flexible status · 48px action. Nothing else may enter a row.

Spacing — one rhythm, six steps.

4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 20 · 24. Inside a card nothing exceeds 16; between cards, 10–12; between page sections, a day-rule and 22. The page stays dense because whitespace is spent between meanings, not inside them.

Hierarchy stops at the barn.

Four levels, ever: section → room → pen → sow. A screen may show at most three of them; the fourth is a navigation, not a nesting. No cards inside cards — a pen group is a ruled table, not a stack of tiles.

What color may mean.

Five statuses own the five hues — nothing else is colored. Olive is not a status: it marks actions, IDs and the full stop. Amber is spent only on "due now." A screen with no urgency is a screen with almost no color, and that is the point.

Voice

Calm · imperative · counted · signed

The register of a good herdsman's notebook: short imperative sentences, numbers before adjectives, no jargon, no exclamation marks. Bad news is stated with the same posture as good news — and everything that happened gets a name and a time.

Empty state
No checks due in this room. NEXT · 14 DUE FRI JUL 9

An empty page still reports the next entry. Quiet is information.

Error
Couldn't save 000268. It's kept on this phone — try again on signal.

Say what is safe before what went wrong. Never blame the glove.

Sync
3 records waiting to sync. NOTHING IS LOST

Sync is a ledger fact, not a spinner. State the count, promise the custody.

Confirm
000318 — pregnant, confirmed. Written to the room record.

The olive full stop closes every confirmation. The mark, working.

Overdue nudge
6 re-checks in Room 2 are past due. OLDEST · +2D

The nudge is a count and an age. No "please", no red prose — the chip carries the heat.

Done
Room 2 done — 200 of 200 checked. SIGNED G. HANSEN · 23:00

Completion is a signature, not a celebration. The record is the reward.

10

Verdict

What it proves · where it strains

Field Ledger bets that trust is Sentri's product — that a farm will forgive density long before it forgives a lie about lateness.

→ Proves

  • The delta row earns its signature status. Scheduled-struck-through beside actual, chip carrying the difference, reads correctly in one glance at arm's length — and it turns the app's dirtiest secret (slippage) into its most trustworthy feature.
  • The ledger table absorbs density better than cards ever did. 56px ruled rows in pen chapters scale to 200 sows with zero added chrome; the reference UI's card-per-row approach does not.
  • Continuity is nearly free. Jakarta and Plex Mono are kept, olive and amber-for-due are retuned rather than replaced — this could roll out as a theme update, not a retraining event.
  • Warm paper survives the barn. The matte ground kills glare-shimmer that pure white suffers in sunlight, and it makes the five status hues read warmer-but-separated rather than fluorescent.

✕ Strains

  • Fraunces is deck candy at phone scale. Below ~20px it smears on cheap wet screens, so the serif survives only in titles and celebrated counts — a thin footprint for a third font family; the direction must prove it's worth the 60KB.
  • Warm paper narrows amber's headroom. #D99A21 on #F6F4EC is the palette's tightest pairing; the dark text-tone rescue works, but "due now" — the load-bearing status — has the least margin of the five, in the worst lighting.
  • Brand olive and status green are neighbors. Separation leans on darkness and role (olive = buttons, green = words), which holds — until a deutan-vision worker meets a small dot with no word beside it. The "never color alone" law is a mitigation, not a cure.
  • Struck-through mono at 10px is fussy. On a late-heavy room the delta chips multiply and the register look tips toward clutter; the pattern needs a "collapse deltas when >30% late" rule the deck asserts but hasn't tested.

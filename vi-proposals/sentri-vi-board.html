01

The idea

The departure board

A farm day is a schedule. Two hundred sows to check, forty-six to breed, twelve farrowing — and one worker deciding, every few minutes, what needs me next. Sentri is the board that schedule is read from.

A departure board earns trust by discipline, not decoration: every flight in its row, every row in the same columns, and one column — status — allowed to shout. Sentri borrows the discipline and leaves the airport behind. Near-white ground, ink facts, mono numerals. Location, task, count, time — all achromatic. Then a single right-aligned rail of colored words, lined up across every row, readable with wet gloves at arm's length in sunlight.

When everything on screen is a row, two hundred animals is not a design problem — it is just more board. And when color can only ever mean status, a worker never has to ask whether a splash of green is a brand flourish or a fact. It is always a fact.

One glance, one columnAll color on the screen lives in the status rail. Triage is a single vertical eye movement down the right edge — never a hunt across the surface.
Everything in its cellMono-forward type gives every ID, count and time a fixed-width cell. Alignment does the work hierarchy usually begs decoration to do.
The delta is the storyScheduled is struck through in ink; actual is colored with the gap — JUL 6 +2D. The board doesn't editorialize; it shows drift.
02

The mark

Three rows · one cell lit
Primary · construction on 4-unit grid

Three rows of work; one cell of signal. The long bars are the board — the facts, in ink, always the same length, always aligned. The rail column sits flush right, one square per row, and exactly one square carries color: the row that needs you. It is the whole identity in six rectangles — the ground stays quiet, the rail speaks.

The lit cell is amber by default — due now, the load-bearing state of a working day. In live surfaces the mark may light red on an active alert and green when the day is clear; it never lights two cells at once. Print and favicon use amber, always.

The wordmark is set in JetBrains Mono at 700 — six characters, six equal cells, one em advance each. The name itself obeys the grid. No container, no gradient, no rounding: the mark survives lamination, ear-tag printers, and 1-bit barn displays without redrawing.

Below 24 px the mark recuts to two rows so the lit cell stays a legible square — small sizes get their own cut, not a shrink.

Typography

Barlow Semi Condensed · JetBrains Mono

Two faces, strict jobs. Barlow Semi Condensed is signage lineage — cut from American highway lettering — compact enough to survive a 390 px row with five facts in it. JetBrains Mono is the board voice: every digit, ID, time and status word lives in a fixed-width cell, so columns align by construction, not by eye.

Display — Barlow Semi Condensed600–700, uppercase, +3–6% tracking. Screen titles, section names, CTAs. Signage voice: short, loud, never long-form.

Check every sow in the pen, then submit the pen as one record. Uncertain results become re-checks automatically — nothing waits on your memory.
Data — JetBrains Mono500–800, tabular by design. IDs, counts, times, pen codes, deltas, the status rail. If it can be compared down a column, it is mono.

Status must survive sunlight.

Glare compresses contrast and kills subtle color first. Three defenses: every rail color holds ≥ 4.5:1 against paper, so the word survives even when the hue washes out; status is always a word, never only a dot, so a worker who cannot resolve color at arm's length can still read the row; and rail words are bold mono caps — thick strokes at small sizes, the same weight glare-tested signage uses. Washes (#FAEAE7 and family) are backgrounds for strips only and never the sole carrier of meaning.

Absolute floor — gloves
D-1
Hierarchy stops at two visible levels. Section → room on Today; pen → animal in a task. Deeper structure is navigation, never indentation.
D-2
Rows, rules, and one panel style. Hairline-separated rows on paper; at most one bordered panel (progress, counter) per screen region. No nested cards, no shadows inside screens.
D-3
Color may mean status. Color may not mean anything else. Not brand, not selection, not emphasis, not delight. CTAs are ink; links are ink underlined; the mark's lit cell is itself a status.
D-4
The rail is sacred space. The rightmost 96 px of any row belongs to the status word and its timestamp. Nothing else may occupy or cross it — chevrons and actions live on the row body.
D-5
Spacing rhythm is 4. All paddings and gaps are multiples of 4 px; row heights land on 50/58 so five to seven rows fill a phone screen above the dock.
D-6
Density scales by repetition, not compression. Two hundred rows use the same 50 px row as twelve. When space runs out, group headers collapse — type never shrinks below 13 px.

Voice

Calm · imperative · counted

The board speaks like a schedule: short declaratives, counts before adjectives, next action always named. Never jargon, never cheer, never blame. If a sentence survives being read once, wet, at arm's length — it ships.

Verdict

What it proves · where it strains
Proves
  • Arm's-length triage genuinely works. One aligned rail, five words, the entire color budget spent at the decision point. Bold mono caps on near-white is the highest-contrast pattern of the three directions — the one most likely to survive glare, gloves and a cracked screen protector.
  • Density is native, not negotiated. 200 sows is the 50 px row ×200; the tabular grammar needs zero new components between a 5-row day and a 500-row audit. This is the direction with the smallest design-system surface to build and maintain.
  • The delta pattern inherits for free. Struck schedule + colored actual reads inside any mono cell — board level (DUE JUL 6 +2D) and animal level (due Jul 8 · done Jul 8) use one convention, no bespoke widget.
  • The color law is enforceable. "Color = status, nothing else" is a rule a team can actually police in review — unlike "use the brand color tastefully." Amber stays load-bearing because nothing else is allowed to be amber.
Strains
  • It runs cold. Achromatic ink and mono read administrative — closer to a terminal than a place someone spends ten hours a day. Warmth has to come entirely from voice and motion, which a static deck cannot prove.
  • The rail is anglophone. "IN PROGRESS" is 11 characters; its Chinese equivalent is 3, its German one 14. Word-shaped status means localization changes the rail's width and its at-a-distance silhouette — the signature degrades in translation unless status vocabulary is engineered per locale.
  • Action surfaces strain the grammar. Farrowing's counters, steppers and big CTA aren't rows; chapter 07 had to borrow a card-and-button language the board doesn't natively supply. Every future form screen will pull against the system the same way.
  • Ownability rests on one gesture. Near-white + ink + mono is the default aesthetic of 2020s dev tools; strip the rail and the mark, and little says Sentri. The identity is distinctive in use but thin in isolation — merch, signage and the app-store tile will have to work hard.

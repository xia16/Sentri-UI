01

The idea

Concept · The instrument cluster

A farrowing barn is not a place you browse software. Gloves, glare, ammonia, two hundred sows and a worker who reads a screen the way a driver reads a dashboard — in half a second, mid-motion, for exactly one thing: does anything need me right now? Sentri answers like an instrument cluster answers. Exceptions pinned to the top in status color. Everything running normally stays quiet gray. Big mono numerals you can read at arm's length, gauge bars segmented like a fuel readout, and a schematic of the barn itself — because on a farm, the address of a problem is a room, not a list index.

The reference lineage is the tractor dashboard, the ward monitor, the flight instrument: machines that people trust with animals and lives precisely because they never decorate. Translated to a sunlit light UI — cool near-neutral housing, white instrument faces, one engineered teal for the controls you operate.

Exception-first, alwaysThe top of every screen belongs to what is wrong or due. The 6 overdue re-checks outrank 2,134 sows that are fine. Quiet is the default state of a healthy barn — and of this UI.
The barn is the interfaceLocation-first drawn literally: a room/pen schematic with live counts is the home surface. Workers think "Gestation, Room 2, pen A3" — the software finally does too.
Numbers are instrumentsEvery numeral in the product is mono and tabular — counts, ear tags, timestamps. A count you glance at twice a minute deserves the fixed pitch of an odometer.
One signal, five statusesGauge teal marks controls and "you are here". Meaning — overdue, due, in progress, done, upcoming — belongs exclusively to the five status hues. The two never trade jobs.

The mark

The pen grid · 100 × 100
Primary · on housing

A room, its pens, and the one that needs you. The outer square is the barn wall; inside, four pens drawn as an equipment schematic; one pen filled solid teal — the sentry's answer. It is the product's whole argument in one glyph: hundreds of animals, one location that matters right now, and you can see which.

Construction is strict: 100-unit grid, 8-unit outer wall, 6-unit pen walls, pens on a 34-unit module. No curves anywhere — the mark is fabricated, not drawn. It reads correctly rotated, embossed on a plastic housing, stenciled on a barn door, or punched into an ear-tag applicator.

Below 24 px the interior simplifies to the small cut: wall plus a single filled quadrant. The filled pen never changes position in any one deployment — like a power lamp, it means the same thing every time you look at it. The wordmark is Barlow Condensed 700, all caps, tracked +6% — the stenciled label on the equipment, not a logotype with a personality of its own.

Typography

Barlow Condensed · Barlow · IBM Plex Mono
Display — Barlow CondensedScreen titles, section names, the wordmark. 600–700, all caps, tracked +2–6%. Barlow's letterforms descend from California highway and equipment signage — mechanical authority is in its DNA, not applied as styling.

Text — BarlowAll interface and body text, 12.5–16px, weights 400–600. Same superfamily as the display face: one industrial voice at two widths, zero font-pairing cleverness. Wide apertures and open counters hold up on wet, scratched glass.
Check each sow and record the result. Sows already confirmed stay gray — only the ones that need a decision carry color. Results save on the device first and sync when the barn has signal.

Why gauge teal, and why it is ownable. Safety-equipment orange was auditioned first and rejected on physics: amber is load-bearing in this system, and in direct sun a scratched screen collapses orange and amber into one hue — the brand would dilute the most important status. Teal at #0E7C8A is the instrument-panel alternative: the hue of coolant lines, surgical drape, and oscilloscope traces — equipment, not marketing. In the livestock-software category, which is wall-to-wall John Deere green and enterprise blue, a high-chroma industrial teal is unclaimed. And it is engineered to keep its distance from meaning: 45° from status blue (which is pushed to indigo), 90° from status green, opposite the warm alarm hues — with white text holding 4.9:1 on the solid.

Status must survive sunlight. Every status exists in three redundant channels — hue, a text label, and position (exceptions top, done bottom). Text-weight variants (#C2402E, #8F6400, #3E56C4, #3B8A50) all clear 4.5:1 on white; washes are backgrounds only, never the sole carrier. A colorblind worker in glare loses nothing but tint.

Voice

Calm · imperative · numbers attached

The panel speaks like a good foreman: short, present tense, a number in every sentence that matters, and no feelings about the data. Never jargon ("gestational confirmation event"), never cheer ("Great job!"), never blame.

Empty state · no exceptions
All quiet. 2,140 sows, nothing due before 14:00.
Quiet is a reading, not an absence — the panel reports zero with the same precision as six.
Error · record failed
Didn't save. Your entry is kept on this phone — try again where there's signal.
Blame the network, keep the data, tell them where it lives. No red unless the animal is affected.
Sync
3 records waiting to sync. They'll go when signal returns.
A count and a promise. Sync is logistics, not drama — amber dot, no modal.
Confirm · destructive-ish
Mark 000302 not pregnant? She moves to the rebreed list for Room 1.
States the consequence as a location change — because in this product, that's what consequences are.
Overdue nudge
6 re-checks overdue in Room 2. Oldest is 2 days late.
Count, place, worst case. Imperative comes from the number, not from an exclamation mark.
Done
Room 2 recorded: 100 of 200 checked. 100 remain for day 2.
Completion is a ledger line, not a celebration. The next shift's number is already on the panel.
10

Verdict

Honest · feeds the compare sheet

What Panel proves

  • The barn schematic is the argument no other direction can make. Location-first stops being a claim in a spec and becomes the literal home screen — the 6 overdue re-checks have a room you can point to and walk to.
  • Exception-first survives density. With 2,140 sows on one farm, the strip-plus-quiet-gray economy keeps the screen honest: color spend tracks urgency exactly, and a healthy barn genuinely looks calm.
  • The all-numerals-mono rule is cheap to build and hard to copy. One CSS-level decision produces the entire instrument feel — tags, counts, gauges align into a single machined texture across every screen.
  • Gauge teal is defensibly ownable. It is absent from the ag-software category, engineered away from all five status hues, and it earns its keep by marking only controls — the brand never competes with an animal's status.

Where it strains

  • The schematic must scale or it lies. Ashfield's 4 sections and 10 rooms fit in 210px; a 12-building integrator site will not. The gesture needs a designed collapse (section chips → room grid) before this identity is credible beyond mid-size farms.
  • Cold at the moments that are not mechanical. A stillbirth counter rendered as odometer digits is honest but heartless; the instrument metaphor has no register for the parts of farrowing that are animal husbandry, not machinery.
  • Condensed caps flirt with shouting. Barlow Condensed headers plus mono labels put a lot of uppercase on small screens; one weight slip and the panel reads as a warning poster. The system depends on discipline the DS file must enforce.
  • Teal's contrast ceiling is real. At #0E7C8A white text passes but never soars; disabled and pressed states need the deep variant, and on amber-washed surfaces teal controls need a bordered treatment. More state-styling rules than Ledger's ink-on-paper.

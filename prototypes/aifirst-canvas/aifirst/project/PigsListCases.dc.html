Pigs list on a treatment: every case
One list for every treatment. A row is a pig with an ID, or a pen's pigs without one; the pen is the farm's label (lane letter + number); the only grouping is the room. Tags and pens are the farm view's own: the sow in B15 is 206950. Ruled = the owner said so · Proposed = my call, not yet seen · Owner to rule = a conflict with a ruling · Codex = added or changed across three rounds of Codex (GPT) attacks: 27 cards. Owner rulings of 8 Nov applied: doses count by their day; withdrawal and sync are not shown here.

Behind the list
ZRules for the developerCodexProposed
Not visible on their own, but every case above depends on them.
  1. Withdrawal on the treatment page is only the plan's estimate; per-pig withdrawal lives on the pig's record.
  2. A pig has an identity under her tags: tags can change or be added, history stays with her.
  3. Each dose has its own list of pigs it was for. Joining or leaving changes later doses, never earlier ones.
  4. A dose on the track is solid once every pig it was for is settled (given, skipped, taken off), not only when all were given.
  5. A record keeps what was actually given: amount, product, route, time, who, and the prescription version it followed.
  6. Corrections are audited and put the wrong pig back to her true state; the same upload twice counts once; two real doses count twice.
  7. Water and feed courses keep each pen's time with medicated access, from first access to the confirmed end (line flushed, or feeder emptied), with interruptions.
  8. Counts are pigs, not rows. Filters and pages never change the course totals.
  9. The order is fixed (walking order, pen, tag) so paging can't skip or repeat a pig.
  10. A recount of an untagged pen is a stock change; it never edits recorded doses.
  11. Each scheduled dose has an identity (medicine, moment, the pigs it is for); selections and filters bind to it, never to a dose number.
  12. Recorded, settled and reviewed are three facts: a partial or wrong-product dose is recorded, not settled, until the vet reviews it.
  13. Untagged pigs keep sub-groups by treatment history under one pen; moves, merges, deaths and new tags carry known counts and mark the rest unknown.
  14. Exposure in water or feed is an interval per pen and per pig, from first access to the confirmed end, intersected with where the pig was. A day counts as delivered only if access ran the whole prescribed period.

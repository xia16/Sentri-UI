The unified convention
Fact (line 1) — words, in two weights. A state the task asks about prints full weight (Due · today); a forecast prints in the light register with the word that admits it — Expected · in 5 days — because the prediction can be wrong and biology overrules it. There is no dim state and nothing is ever disabled: tag and checkbox stay full strength on every row, and an early mark on an Expected row is legal. Done rows differentiate by ✓ · stamp · ✎, never by grey.
Fact (line 1) — words. The animal's cycle position as a readable phrase — space is free on line 1, so spend it on clarity: Mated 21 days ago · Due in 5 days · Signs · 10h ago. Past events read elapsed, scheduled read until.
Metadata (line 2) — tokens. Space is scarce, so compress: mono, lowercase, · separated tokens (started 6h · parity 3), fixed order time → counts → codes. Open rows carry only relative times and counts — no clock stamps. Two registers, one rule: words where there is room, tokens where there is not.
Stamps. Jul 8 · 07:14 · G.H · JY001 are record trails: they live on done rows, and on an open row's metadata line when its state descends from a prior record (signs 10h · G.H, unclear Jul 5 · G.H). Working judgments stay elapsed; records stay absolute.
Chip law. A chip is the reason this row needs you first — an escalated measurement (LAST 45M), a flagged observation recorded by a hand (SIGNS), or a standing instruction the walk itself executes (+0.4 KG · NO FEED — an individual feed act, check-in 06d). Below threshold, measurements stay grey tokens; the flag's trail (who · when) lives in the metadata line. Picker cells: the pen's one statement. Nothing else is a chip — filter options and lens segments are controls; done outcomes are grey text + ✓; peek mirrors the row.
Rail. Box tasks: empty — the bar acts. Rail tasks: verdict word + › — the tap acts. Done: ✎.
Template. Two lines, every row, every task — one height, one rhythm across the product.
Lens law. Segments name the pig's state — never an action verb — using the most specific truthful word: a biological state where one exists (Not in heat / In heat, No return / Returned, Open / Mated, Awaiting / Active), and Unchecked / Checked as the floor where completion produces a record, not a state (pregnancy's three outcomes, postpartum, weaning). Consistency lives in position, not vocabulary: the first segment is always the working pile, the second always the finished pile, All always last. Length rules: copy budget ≤ 12 characters at 3 segments, ≤ 8 at 4 (中文 never binds); segments hug their content instead of splitting into equal thirds; when space still runs out, inactive segments drop their counts — the active one keeps its; four states is the ceiling — a fifth is a sub-lens or a filter dimension, never a segment; ellipsis on a status word is banned.

05 · The entrance — how an event is reached

Selection names the subject · the sheet holds the verbs
What this section settles

04 is how an event is written; this is how it is reached. Production scattered one catalogue across ten entry points — 更多操作, three parent-function drawers, per-row overflow, per-screen shortcuts — so no two tasks agreed where an action lived. Here there is one: selection names the subject, and a sheet holds the verbs.

Three drafts died getting here and are worth recording. A pig drawer organised by domain was cut because the farmer already knows the verb before opening the app — she saw the symptom, she saw the pregnancy loss, and no screen changes that. Promotion in unit mode was cut because a walkthrough has no verb to promote: what you find is the point. And domain grouping inside the sheet was cut on its own arithmetic — once blocked verbs are omitted, health is the only domain that ever holds more than two, so the rules were drawn around singletons.

Six rules of the sheet
Verbs only. No state, no facts, no counts — the farmer decided before opening it, and the context that makes an entry safe belongs in the record sheet's own header (04), where it changes what gets typed.

One tile size, everywhere. Tiles sit in a fixed five-column grid, so a strip holding three verbs leaves two slots empty instead of stretching to fill them. Stretching made a three-tile strip look like a different component from a five-tile one; a fixed grid keeps every strip the same kind of thing, and a strip's length becomes a reading of what this subject allows.

Tiles, and pills where a tile cannot work. A verb becomes a tile when its name survives shortening to one word and a glyph can support recognition — Condition · Treatment · Triage · Resolve · Death · Weigh · Backfat · Transfer · Tag · Note. It becomes a pill when it cannot: no glyph exists for a cycle event (production used 💀 for 流产 and 分娩 alike, one a pregnancy loss and one a birth), and Pregnancy is not Pregnancy result. Pills run two-up; a lone or odd-last pill spans the full width, so there is never a gap. Nothing moves between tiles and pills to balance a layout — the form has to keep meaning what it means.

Three strips, always named — Health · Routine · Production — and a strip with nothing in it is absent, not empty. A pen of growers has no cycle, so it has no Production strip at all.

The PEN strip leads when a pen is ticked, and strip labels carry their scope. A ticked pen contributes itself and its pigs, so the sheet reads PEN · HEALTH — 13 PIGS · … — the verb declares its subject, and no verb is ambiguous: Condition is only ever about animals, Fault only ever about the pen. Mixed selections are legal (2 pens · 29 pigs); pen verbs scope to the ticked pens, pig verbs to all ticked pigs. Feed is never a verb here — the pen's number is a sum, and an adjustment is always the consequence of a case (Thin · Fat, bulk-markable), never a direct act.

Death is the last health verb. A farmer who finds a dead pig is thinking about health, not about herd accounting, and the sheet should match that rather than a taxonomy. It is a tile like the others — same size, same grid — with a red outline and no fill: the only unfilled tile on the sheet. An earlier draft made it small grey text under a rule, which conflated two needs: a piglet death is a daily event and must be easy to find; what it must be is hard to hit by accident. Distinct styling does both; shrinking it did neither.

Blocked verbs are omitted, not greyed, and one mono line accounts for them (4 not available for a gilt ›). Three rows saying not bred is noise in the best space; one line loses nothing, because tapping it lists them with their reasons. This revises the greyed-with-reason rule in pig-actions.html, written when abortion was one row in a seven-row menu rather than one of four dead rows in thirteen. Order is fixed and never learned; arity rides a pill as a trailing token (one by one), and a tile says it in the sheet it opens.
On the glyphs

The marks above are stand-ins chosen to render in a browser, not the drawn set. What the specimens fix is which verbs get a glyph at all and how much work it may do: recognition only, never meaning — every tile keeps its word. Icon design is its own pass and its first constraint is already known, because production's own set failed it.

The pig page is a detail page — anatomy, rules and the rendered specimen live in 07, beside its siblings (the pen sheet, batch composition, task detail). Reached by tapping her row; its ⋯ raises the verb sheet with a subject of one.

Check-in
What it asserts, precisely. Every condition, treatment and heat mark was written the moment it was recorded — so the check-in cannot be a submission, and calling it one would contradict the law that actions commit themselves. The only thing left to claim is the negative: I covered this unit and there was nothing further to report. That is the one fact the system cannot derive, and it is the whole compliance story. It stamps unit · person · time, and it can be repeated.
Why "check-in" and not "inspection"

巡检 translates as inspection, and production ends it with a green 提交巡检 — submit inspection. Both words carry a formality the act does not have. Submit implies data is being sent, when it was all committed during the walk. Inspection implies a verdict on the pigs, when the verdict is whatever the recorded events say. What is actually being claimed is presence: a named person covered this unit at this time.

That is attendance, so the vocabulary follows it: the header reads checked 09:41 · G. Hansen, Today lists units not checked today, and the button says Check-in — the act happens at the door, on leaving, and the word finishes the walk rather than starting one. The name for the surface stays the check-in.

One thing to watch. A header target is a poor thumb reach, and this is the walk's one deliberate act. It happens once per unit, at the door, and a person is putting their name to it — so a deliberate reach may be right rather than a flaw. Worth testing before it is settled.

How arity is decided, and what it settles for free

Arity is a property of the event, not of a screen: ask whether the payload is the same for every subject. Same → Bulk. Different → Conveyor. Meaningless beyond one → Single. The per-event assignment lives with the catalogue in product-model.html §4.

Death lands as a Conveyor because cause and photos are per animal — so a bulk death is N sheets, and the friction that protects it is structural rather than a warning dialog bolted on. That is a consequence of the model, not a decision anyone made.

Seven rules of the detail page
It reads; it never acts. Acting is the verb sheet's job (⋯ on a pig's page, the tick elsewhere); a chevroned line opens a record, and records act — the fault's own sheet is where Resolve lives.

The fact line: words left, trail right. Left is the fact in sentence words; right is the mono trail — when · who · qualifier. The same two registers as the row (01b), rotated into a page.

Sections are keyed, and an empty section is absent. The zero rule again: her Litter section does not render as “none”, it does not render.

The tail is history — or the end-act. Living subjects (pig, pen, batch) end in their event trail, capped with all ›. A task ends in End task, because a task is the one subject that finishes.

The page outlives the subject. Scanning the tag of a pig that has left opens her page, not a selection — there is nothing to act on, so no verb sheet rises and no verbs render. The terminal band leads (✕ left the herd · died aug 02), the state sections freeze at her final state, and history remains whole. “On farm” is never a field; departure is a state the page wears.

The registry lives one tap in, not in tabs. Identity & provenance › opens the clerk's page — tag, notch, breed, genetic line, DoB, entrance, arrival, sire and dam, other IDs — and the pen's Capacity & silo › is its twin. The main page stays ordered by volatility: what changes daily on top, what changes never behind the chevron, history last. Tabs would be a new component for one use; the chevron row is the grammar we already have.

02 · Pen picker — one component, every task

Map = truth · tap = land · long-press = peek · never acts · swipe →
How the picker works

The map of the unit, for one question: is there work for me here, how much, how urgent. A cell is a pen code and, at most, one chip — the same chip the rows use for important facts. Chips exist only for states that ask for a hand: red overdue, amber attention (signs · due today · interval), green ready. Green means go; done is a ✓ beside the pen code with the outcome in muted text (6 mated · 11 born · 4 in heat) — a glyph, not a colour, so green never means two things. States that ask nothing (wait 9h · due 3d · next 2d) are grey text; farrowing active is a neutral chip. Counts lead the word (3 SIGNS) and appear only from 2; durations always carry a unit. Text is ink; only chips carry colour; no legend. 75% sheet, 24 pens without scrolling; wider units pan with the column header fixed.

Rules: truth · land · never acts · peek
The map is truth; the lens is a view. The picker never inherits the lens or the filter — every pen shows its real state. A dimmed cell means exactly one thing: no pigs from this task in that pen. It is never a side-effect of what you filtered.
Tap = land. Sheet closes, the list scrolls so that pen's header is pinned at the top, rows beneath. If the current lens hides what you came for, the pinned header says so and flips the lens in one tap (PEN D3 · 4 in heat · show in "In heat" ›). Dimmed pens are the only ones that would need "All", and no one taps those in a task — so the picker never has to reach it.
The grid never acts. Considered and rejected: cell-level quick actions. They work for pen-level tasks (piglet processing, weaning) and are ambiguous for individual ones (which sow?), so some cells would commit and some would not — every tap becomes a question, and a mis-tap in gloves becomes a wrong record instead of a wrong scroll. Pen-level quick actions live in the pinned pen header of the table instead (PEN A2 · 8 piglets · Iron done): one tap after landing, on the surface that already commits, with the litter visible.
Long-press = peek. Hold a cell ~350ms and a small read-only card lists the pen's rows (tag · status); release and it's gone. No checkboxes, no actions, never required — an accelerator for "which three are ready?" before walking. Plain pointer timing, no native component.

How the peek works

Hold a cell ~350 ms and a card rises from it: the pen's rows exactly as the table renders them — same component, read-only: no checkbox, no › — capped at five with +N more. Anything you want in the peek, you put in the row; there is one place to manage. Release and it is gone; the picker underneath is untouched. Nothing in the card can be tapped, ticked or committed: it is a look before walking, not a screen. Tap the pen instead to work it. Built from an ordinary positioned card and a press timer; the tap-to-land fires only if the press ends before the timer, so the two never collide.

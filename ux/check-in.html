The check-in

The walk. One unit, pen by pen — deliver the feed, look at the animals, mark what needs doing. The only surface that watches what nothing is scheduled to watch.

Three documents. components.html holds the parts and the laws, screens.html the task instances, and this one the check-in. Section numbers are shared across all three, so a reference like (01b) or (05) resolves wherever it lives. Nothing here is a new component — the row, the chip, the lens row, the dock, the verb sheet and the pen picker are the ones already built, configured for a walk.

06 · The screen

Strips are places · cards are animals · the right edge is the route
What a check-in is, and why it is not a task

All eight production tasks are sow-cycle or litter cohorts, scoped by batch. A grower on day 68, a nursery pen, a gestating sow at day 87, a boar — no task will ever list them, and their entire visibility in the product is this screen. That decides the design problem: it is not what to put on a row, it is what to keep off, because a unit is 220 animals and the eight that matter must be visible without reading the other 212.

The walk braids two jobs that look opposite. A routine instruction that applies to every pen — deliver this much here — and an exception report that applies to almost none. The strip carries the route; the cards carry the exceptions.

A pen is a card; its animals are rows inside it. Containment does the separating — the old UI had this right. The pen's header row carries a chip code (well-tinted, like the record sheet's pen chip), the census, and the quiet right slot; pigs are two-line rows within. Ground shows between cards, which is where the breathing room comes from — the full-bleed banding was the noise.
The right edge is the route. Every feed amount is right-aligned in one column — the same slot task mode gives 4 OF 6 LEFT — so a farm hand with a cart reads today's numbers straight down. The far-right token is always today's value.
A pen has three states, and the tail row is the door between them. Quiet → one line. Something to see → it opens itself showing only those rows, ending in a centred ⌄ 11 more control. The tail (or the chevron) → the full pen, every animal, physical order; the chevron again → collapsed. The strip's body is not the door between states — it opens the pen sheet. A 20-pen unit stays ~25 lines, and “let me look at all of them” is always one tap. Collapsing is safe only because scan reaches any pig whether or not its row is rendered (04c · 05) — we hide rows, never pigs.

Three rules of the strip
The header is one line, and the card is the pen. A 22px tick, the chip code, the census, the quiet right slot — no second line, no sentence text; anything that would need one lives on the pen sheet. Rows and the tail sit inside the card, and ground separates the cards. Three zones, one law: left edge acts, body reads, right edge discloses. The tick (left 56px) selects the pen and its pigs — the verb sheet rises with a PEN strip leading (05). The body opens the pen sheet — the pen's story: today's feed derived, faults, count trail, history — mirroring the pig row's tap-to-read. The chevron (right 56px) expands and collapses; auto-opened pens show their news plus the ⌄ n more tail, which completes them.
Every slot token is self-describing. A number carries its unit (47.6 kg), a census its noun (14 pigs), a formula its name (Grower 1, never a code letter), a mode its word (ad-lib, station-fed). Formula names are farm-configured, so the constraint runs upstream: the barn never abbreviates a name; the console enforces a short display name at authoring (~14 characters) — the same pattern as the lens copy budget, with the cut made where someone can choose the words rather than where a glove has to read them. This is the same contract task mode's aggregate already keeps — 4 of 6 left explains itself — so the strip is one component with one grammar in both modes.
The feed slot holds a value, or a transition into today's value. Quiet: 18.2 kg. Stepped: 47.2 → 47.6 — from what was poured yesterday to what is poured today, self-contained, in the direction of the action. Ad-lib pens show the word ad-lib, and Nursery 2 → Grower 1 on the day a formula switches; individually-fed pens show nothing, because the station pours, not the hand. Only steps speak — daily drift never prints.
Faults are an icon. A wrench and a count, production's own signal given the strip's scale. Device names, reporter and times live inside the pen. Deliberately absent from the strip: designations (hospital is emergent — the red dots inside are the fact), affirmative batch, override counts, initials.
The pen's own verbs are four. Report fault (Single — a fault names a device in this pen) · Set count (Single, untagged pens only) · Pen note (Bulk) · Feed — one door for everything feed: see today's, adjust the pen by %, switch the formula (pre-filled on due days; Bulk across a batch's pens from the unit sheet). Feed levels: the pen adjusts as a whole; a single pig adjusts from her own verb sheet (N=1 only); bulk per-pig plans come from marking Thin / Fat, each carrying her banded plan. A shared percentage across different ticked animals is deliberately impossible — that is the pen's job.

No verb is ambiguous, so the selection never asks what you meant. Condition is only ever about animals; Fault only ever about the pen. One sheet carries both, each strip labelled with its subject and count — which is what no bar label could say.
Ticking a pen contributes the pen and its pigs. “Pen-level add condition” is one gesture: tick C3, then Condition under HEALTH — 13 PIGS. 06c · Feed

One slot · a value, or a transition into today's value
Why feed lives on the strip and not in a task

Feed is not scheduled work with its own list — the farm hand feeds while walking, so the walk carries the number. That makes every pen's amount routine rather than exceptional: it sits in the strip's right slot as the route, never in a chip. The value moves daily (effective_day advances, week anchors interpolate) but nobody re-dials a feeder for thirty grams — so the slot reports steps, not drift, as a transition: 47.2 → 47.6. The rejected forms: 47.6 ↑0.4 puts the delta far-right and breaks the route column; a bare arrow loses the magnitude a feeder is re-dialled by.

Record the exception, not the compliance. Delivering the prescribed amount is the default and needs no act. Only a deviation is recorded — less because the trough was still full, more because it was cleaned out. The useful half of variance data, without a tap on a normal day.

Individual adjustments resolve into the pen. A sow on +10% in a trough-fed pen cannot be fed separately — she eats with everyone — so her adjustment is already inside the pen's total. The strip does not say so; the farm hand pours one number. Thin is a case on her line 1; the plan is its treatment. If the walker must do it — a top-dressed scoop, a pre-farrow withhold — it is an amber chip with the amount (+0.4 kg · no feed) and the pen opens itself; if it happens anyway (the station, or the pen total already contains it) it is a grey note (feed +10%). The Feed filter is simply the subset with feed news; the pen sheet holds the full derivation.

Nothing to deliver, and the strip says why. An ESF station applies each sow's ration itself, adjustments included. Overrides are standing facts read inside the pen or under the Feed filter — never an alarm.
Stage-mixed pens have no defined answer. Gilts and sows in one pen means two curves; the feed model has only a sex-mixing rule, which resolves to the more demanding class and quietly over-feeds the rest. Because we derive the count from placement events we do know each animal's stage, so summing per animal into a pen total is exact — but it is a claim the feed model does not support. Flagged, not assumed.

06d · The pig row

Two tokens on line 2, and everything else is one tap in
The creme-de-la-creme rule

The bar for the row is: does it change what the walker does at walking speed. The case and its day, the dot, an escalated measurement, one standing token, and the open trail all pass — the trail because treated 3d is what decides tap-in versus walk-on. Cut to the inside: initials (audit, not judgment), affirmative batch (the zero rule — a token that says “normal” is not printed; only no batch appears, because it means nothing else in the product will ever show this pig), full histories, characteristics, and every unescalated measurement.

Line 2 is at most: standing · no batch · trail — fixed order, missing slots collapse, never reorder. The standing token is exactly one: age for a gilt, parity for a sow, head count for a litter. The trail slot holds only what has been done, in past tense (treated 3d) — pending is the dot itself, and a dot with no done-trail is the unexecuted mark. An active feed plan prints as its mechanism (feed +10%) beneath the Thin case it treats. no batch prints only when no task is watching her: a weaned sow inside the service window is a station, not an orphan.

06e · Triage — a mark, not an act

Colour carries rank · words carry identity
Why this is the one place colour may replace a word

An earlier attempt to colour-code statuses failed because those statuses are categorical — in heat, mated, returned — and a categorical colour needs a legend, which was the complexity we cut. Triage is ordinal. Red › amber › none reads as more-urgent-to-less without anyone being taught it, so the dot needs no legend and no word.

Two solid dots — red above orange — and what that costs. A ring variant was tried for colour-vision safety and read as a third state, which is worse than the risk it hedged; solid pairs won. The dot is never the sole carrier — the case and its day are always words, an urgent pig usually also carries a chip, and the Attention filter sorts red first — but red against orange alone is a known colour-vision weakness. If barn testing shows confusion, the fallback is size, not shape. Strip text sits at body-ink contrast, never the light annotation grey: the strip is an instruction surface, not a caption.

And it frees the chip. Triage and an escalated measurement previously competed for one slot, so a sow who was both urgent and running 40.6° could only say one of them.

06f · Cases and characteristics

An abnormality is not a condition

A hernia, a blind eye, a missing ear do not open and close. They just are, like parity or sex. Treating one as a condition is what produces Hernia day 340 on a row — noise pretending to be news.

Each disease and symptom entry carries its class, so recording a hernia produces a characteristic and recording a fever produces a case with nobody choosing — the derive-everything law (01b). A characteristic can spawn a case. A hernia that ruptures, a chronic bad leg that goes acute — that is a new case referencing the standing characteristic, not a change of state on it. Both records stay honest.
A long-running case is still a case. What makes something a case is its catalogue class plus the fact that someone is managing it — never elapsed time. Otherwise a fever nobody treated for three weeks would quietly reclassify itself into a fact of life, which is the exact failure the walk exists to catch.

The act can carry the readings, so they stop needing a door. Production collects 环境数据 on its submit screen; we collect the same two measures on the finish sheet — the one moment the farm hand is already saying “I am done here.” Unit note rides along. All of it is optional: skipping every field and tapping Check-in is a complete, valid check-in — the assertion was never the readings, it is the covered-and-nothing-further stamp.
Not a submission. Every condition, treatment and mark was written the moment it was recorded; nothing on this sheet gates or bundles them. The sheet exists because the stamp and the readings share a moment, not because anything needs collecting.
Unit-wide acts live here too. Switch feed runs the transition across every pen of a batch in one act — pre-filled when due, a formula picker when ad hoc — because a switch is Bulk and the unit sheet is where the whole unit is the subject. Per-pen remains possible from any strip.
The ⋯ is gone — this sheet is the unit's own. Totals and the stamp live in the header's right column; the stamp is the door. Opened mid-walk, the sheet records a note, a reading, or opens the unit-wide equipment list without finishing — Check-in is simply its commit. One surface fewer, and nothing the ⋯ held is homeless.

06h · The pen sheet

The strip instructs · the sheet explains — a detail page (07)
What it is, and how it is reached

The pen's story: why today's number is what it is, what is broken, who set the count, what happened here. An instance of the detail page (07) — read-only, keyed sections, history tail. Reached by tapping the strip's body (left edge acts, body reads, right edge discloses); the wrench and the feed slot land anchored on their sections. Deliberately absent: the pigs list (expansion is that), verb tiles (tick → verb sheet), batch composition (the cards own it).

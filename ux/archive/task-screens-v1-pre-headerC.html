Sentri · Unified task screens · working render Synthesis: Panel structure · Ledger temperature · Board discipline

One list, three tasks.

The unified task list rendered through the synthesized VI, across the three most different tasks it must serve: a sweep with bulk marking (heat check), a per-sow sequence with payload actions (breeding), and long-running records with live alerts (farrowing). Statuses are the production set only, refined in copy. Color appears exactly where a status is stated — nowhere else. All numerals are mono. Nothing is saved, submitted or completed on a room screen — actions commit themselves.

Task contextOne line, display only, above the room

The strip under the app bar is the whole task, not this room: 62 / 100 in heat across 3 rooms, gauge toward the batch target. It exists so a worker in Unit B sees Unit A push the task over the line — awareness without notification. Tapping it opens the task detail sheet (05); the strip is the only door to it, so no Details button. It never carries frequency or KPIs — those don't change what a hand does at this pen.

Room statusThe lens is the room's progress

Scope stacks top-down: the title and strip are the task; a hairline; then the unit — its name set large like a room sign, "Last checked 07:31 · G. Hansen" as a plain sentence (verb stated, never a bare timestamp), and the lens as the unit's status. Task → unit → pens → pigs, never interleaved. No section names anywhere in the header — "Breeding" is a task, not an address.

Bottom barTwo states, one bar: navigate or act

Idle, the bar is navigation: ‹ › walk pen to pen, the wide current-pen button says where you are and opens the pen picker (04b) for a leap, then search and scan. Select anything and the same bar becomes the mark-as actions; clear the selection and navigation returns at the same pen.

Inside a penSticky header, collapse the rest

Pen headers stick while their rows scroll; arriving at a pen (arrow or picker) lands at its top with other pens collapsed, so 16 rows are the only thing under the thumb. A specific sow inside a pen is what search and scan are for — no third navigator; the row rail (04) is the stall-room variant of this same dock, not an addition.

No modeSelection is always available

Being in the task is the selection context: boxes are permanently visible, there is no "Select" button and no long-press. Ticking the first box swaps the bar from navigation to actions; ✕ (or unticking the last box) swaps it back.

ActionsThe bar speaks the task's statuses

Heat check has exactly two markable outcomes, so both fit as buttons — primary is the one that advances the work. A task with more outcomes than fit would show a single "Mark as…" opening one action sheet. Never a new status, never a custom control.

After markingMarked sows change lens, instantly

On confirm the sows move to "In heat" and every count updates — list, lens, pen chips, task strip — from the same derived numbers. Undo is an edit from the "In heat" lens, any time. Nothing is pending, nothing is saved later: the mark is the record.

No save, no submit, no completeThree buttons that do not exist here

Every action commits itself, so Save would imply the opposite. Submit was a round — a concept the product does not have. Complete belongs to the task, one level up; rooms are never completed. The bottom edge holds navigation or actions, nothing else.

Scan = selectThree ways to say which pig, one way to say what

Checkbox, scan and search all do the same thing: put a sow into the selection. Scanning 000319 scrolls her pen into view, ticks her box, tints the row for a moment with a small "scanned" mark, and the dock swaps to the action bar — identical to a tapped box. Scan the next sow and the count grows; tap an action and the marks commit, bar clears, dock returns.

Why not a scan screenThe list stays the surface

A dedicated "scanned pig" page would be a fourth way to mark, with its own buttons and states. Landing in the list instead means the farmer sees the sow in her pen context, her neighbours, and the same two buttons they already know. Scan-scan-scan-Mark is a loop that never leaves the screen.

Dock rebuilt48px everywhere; scan is the biggest thing

The footer text labels were 10px mono — unusable in gloves. Now the dock is one row: pen chips scroll on the left, search and scan are 48px chips pinned right, scan filled ink because it is the action farmers reach for most. Grid dropped — chips + scan + search cover navigation; a pen-map returns only if a room's shape ever demands it.

Tasks without checkboxesScan opens the record

Breeding and farrowing have no bulk marks, so there scan skips selection and opens the sow's sheet directly — the only action that exists for her. Same tool, same place in the dock, resolves to whatever the task's one action is.

Why it failedThree things, one weight

Title, strip and unit block were stacked at near-equal visual rank; the eye had no first stop. Each version below picks a single answer to "what is this screen?" and demotes everything else — hierarchy is the decision of what to make small.

A · Unit-firstThe room sign

Unit 4 is the page: 28px, top-left, unmistakable — this is where you stand. Task name and last-checked demote to one subtitle sentence. Task progress lives in a slim capsule above the title, peripheral, always there. Best when workers think "I'm in Unit 4 doing heat check". Cost: task identity is small.

B · Task-first, switchable unitThe tool with a room dial

Heat check is the page; Unit 4 is a real control (tap ⌄ to switch units without leaving the task) carrying its own last-checked; task progress is one thin line above the lens. Best when a worker sweeps several units in one task session. Cost: three rows above the lens again — but each is a different kind of thing (title / control / meter).

C · One barTask title, then unit | task

Title row is the task: Heat check, with "Last checked 07:31 · G. Hansen" under it. The bar holds two scopes side by side — Unit 4 left carrying the unit's one headline KPI (70% heat rate, target ≥ 90% as a mono suffix; the in-heat count stays in the lens so nothing repeats), Task overview › right (62 / 100 in heat, task gauge, chevron to the sheet). Unit first because it is where you stand; "overview" names the right cell as a door.

KPI ruleOne per task, unit-scoped, target beside it

Each task has a single number a worker legitimately tracks — heat rate, pregnancy rate, live per litter, on-time %, avg wean weight — and it lives here, in the unit cell. Never a second KPI; the rest is the sheet. Ink by default; the number turns amber only when off-target, so the status color law still holds.

My readC for density, A for clarity

C is the cleanest structure — every scope has a place and nothing repeats. A is the most legible at arm's length. B earns its keep only if switching units in-place is a real behaviour. If you want one: C, with A's larger unit type.

Why V1Signs deserves more than a badge

Heat signs is the pre-state of the event this whole task exists to catch — the amber time badge alone gives it the same visual rank as "no data." The 4px edge strip (from the 巡检 v1 design) weights the row at the periphery: scannable down the left edge like the READY rail is down the right, without adding a single word. State word tints amber only on strip rows — color still means status, nothing else.

Strip semanticsEdge = "this row wants eyes"

Amber strip: signs. Green strip: in window (actionable today). No strip: upcoming — quiet rows stay quiet. Gray strip: no data. If every row had a strip the edge would mean nothing; upcoming staying bare is what makes the amber loud.

Why V2Task context with presence

The same one fact as the base strip — 62 / 100 for the whole task — but as a full-bleed header in the VI (warm ground, hairline, no card, no dark surface): a big mono metric and the gauge. More presence for the number that tells Unit B it can ease off; costs ~40px of list. Base vs V2 is a surface decision only — identical content.

Branch workflowBase is never edited in place

01/01b stay canonical. Variations live in V-sections beside their base; when one wins, the file is snapshotted to ux/archive/task-screens-vN.html, the winner merges into the base section, and the branch section is removed. Every iteration stays on disk. The 巡检 v1 reference file is read-only — nothing there was touched.

Variation ruleNo checkbox when the action carries payload

Confirming a mating records a semen batch and a sequence position — that cannot be a bulk mark, so rows have no boxes; the row opens the mating sheet (timeline, batch confirm, interval warning). The rule is mechanical: checkbox iff the bar can fully express the mark.

RailREADY / WAIT is the whole triage

The sequence status ("Mating 2 · ready", "Mating 2 in 9h") reads left; the rail carries the one-word verdict, Board-style, aligned down the screen. Countdown rows are dimmed — visible, not actionable. The 5h interval warning rides the row as an amber badge, stated as a measurement, exactly like the production sheet does.

Bottom barNavigation only — there is nothing to bulk

Each mating commits in its own sheet, so the bar never has an action state here; it is the nav dock permanently: pen chips, search, scan, grid. Scan matters most on this task — doses in hand, the sow in front of you, one tap to her sheet. The task strip above is queue-shaped: mated / to mate, no gauge, because the goalpost moves.

Second lineOne memory-line grammar

Fixed format on every row: n of N · last <time> (or in heat <time> before mating 1; batch replaces "last" once done). Same fields, same order — and because every breeding row carries the line, all rows on the screen are the same height. A screen either uses the memory line everywhere or not at all.

LensFour states, still one grammar

Farrowing partitions into Awaiting / Farrowing / Done — the production statuses grouped, nothing invented. "Farrowing" is the default lens when anything is active: the watch list. Awaiting holds the due/overdue/no-data rows in the same anatomy.

Live rowsThe row is the monitor

An active sow's row carries the running count and the interval measurement — the amber badge is the same "45 min since last piglet" the record sheet shows, surfaced where the worker glances. Same dockThe bar is the same; the room shape adds one strip

Pen rooms get chips. Stall rooms — 4 rows × 100 single-sow stalls — get chips for the row letter plus a rail for the stall number. Nothing else on the screen changes; the dock is one component that renders by room shape, and it still swaps to the action state the moment something is selected.

Two axesLetter picks the row, rail picks the stall

Gestation rooms are rows of single-sow stalls — the "one pig, one pen" case. Letter chips (A–D) switch rows; the rail below represents stalls 1–100. Drag the thumb — the mono bubble reads C·67 as it moves — or tap anywhere on the rail to jump straight there. Reaching C67 from A1 is two gestures, no scrolling through B.

The rail is a mapAmber ticks show where work is

Ticks mark stalls still holding work in the current lens; clusters read as "two groups mid-row, one near the end." The rail answers both "where am I" and "where next" — the same derived numbers as the chips and pen headers, drawn spatially.

Stall rowsStall leads, tag follows

In stall rooms the location is the row's first mono cell (C67), the ear tag dimmed beside it — location-first even at the row level, because the worker stands at stalls, not at tags. Group headers aggregate the row letter. Same anatomy otherwise.

Custom, simplyNo native components

Chips, a bar, ticks, a thumb — all ordinary elements with a drag handler; 44px touch height throughout. The small-room chip strip and this rail are the same slot: a room renders one or the other by its shape.

Two gesturesWalk with arrows, leap with the picker

In the barn you either move to the next pen or jump to a specific one. The dock now serves exactly those two: ‹ › step along the physical route with zero aiming, and the wide current-pen button — PEN A2 · 4 of 6 left, always telling you where you are — opens this picker for the leap. The scrolling chip strip tried to do both and did neither well.

The pickerRow letter, then pen number

Big segmented letters for the row (A B C), then a grid of that row's pens — 58px cells, 8px gaps, mono pen code with its remaining count or ✓. Two taps from anywhere to anywhere; no scrolling for a 24-pen room, one short scroll for the largest. Stall rooms use the same sheet with the rail (04) as the number step.

Mis-tapsReversible by construction

Arrows move one pen and the header names where you landed — a wrong tap is undone by the opposite arrow. Picker cells are glove-sized and separated; a wrong pen is a fresh picker tap away. Nothing in navigation commits anything.

Dock, final shape‹ · current pen · › · ⌕ · ▣

Five things, all 48px, one row, every task. Selection swaps the whole row for the action bar; clearing brings it back at the same pen.

The filterOne question decides the strip vs the sheet

Does this change what a hand does at the pen right now? Only whole-task progress passes — so only that is on the room screen. KPI, configuration, SOP, status legend and End task all fail it, and all live here, one tap away. The sheet exists so nothing has to leak upward.

StructureProduction's 任务详情, compressed

Same sections as production, in the same order — progress, KPI, configuration, SOP, legend, end — but as a bottom sheet over the room, not a sibling tab: the worker never leaves the pen list. Numbers are mono and large; labels are quiet; SOP and legend are links, not inlined blocks. Per-room split (8 · 31 · 23) is the one addition, because it is how Unit B knows Unit A is done.

End taskWhere completion actually lives

Bordered red, at the bottom of the sheet — reachable, never accidental, and one level above any room. Sweep tasks show it always; standing tasks (heat check) show it as the supervisor's stop; event tasks (breeding, farrowing) never show it — they close themselves record by record.

CopyNames the batch, not "task"

Header reads "Batch wk 32 · day 1 of 7". "Task" was a system word; the batch is what the farmer calls it. The room strip drops the word too — "62 / 100 in heat · 3 rooms" is unambiguous once the destination is this sheet.

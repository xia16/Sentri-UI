Consolidating at the node

We have gone bottom-up: ~290 production screens, every action, every requirement. This is the consolidation — what to standardise, what is genuinely atypical, and what to design next. It deliberately does not go inside individual features.

1 · The approach

Now that the capability map is complete, the mistake would be to design 22 features. The right move is to find how few interaction shapes those 22 events actually take, design those, and treat each feature as a configuration. Three matrices get us there — and the third is the one nobody has written yet.

The UI question is nearly solved. The semantic question is not. "Where is the button" is answered by the product model (subject decides surface). What is not answered is what an event does downstream — and your back-door case is exactly this: a pig recorded pregnant during 孕检, found not-pregnant during an inspection. Two records, one animal, opposite facts. Nothing in either production file handles that. It is the highest-value thing to settle at this node, because it decides data model, not pixels.

2 · Matrix 1 — event × archetype

Classified by interaction shape, not by domain meaning. The result: one core component covers two thirds of the product.

The Record Sheet — 15 of 22 events A sheet with a subject header and 0..N fields drawn from a fixed kit: radio group · multi-select picker · stepper · number+unit · segmented scale · text · entity picker · photo grid. Zero fields is a confirm; one radio is a choice; several fields is a form. These are not different components, they are the same sheet with different field lists — and every one of them commits itself, per our existing law.

Shared vs task-owned — the distinction you asked for. Every event is shared; none belong to a task. What differs is whether the task asked for it: • The task's own event — the sheet you get by tapping the row. Recording it advances the task. • Any other event — reached through ⋯ on the same sheet. Recorded against the animal; the task is unaffected unless it happens to be watching that event too. Marking a pig dead or diseased mid-task is not a special case — it is the second row of the same menu she would show during an inspection.

Supersession — the back-door rule When a new event contradicts an earlier one about the same fact, the later observation wins and the earlier record is superseded, never deleted. The animal's state, batch membership and task progress all re-derive from the current truth; the superseded record stays in her history with its original attribution, marked as such. Your case — recorded pregnant in 孕检, found not pregnant during a walk — becomes: record the not-pregnant event, she leaves the batch, the pregnancy task's counts re-derive, and her history shows both records. No dialog asks the farmer to reconcile anything; he just records what he sees. This is also what 移除 vs 康复 is really about, and your definition confirms it: 移除 = supersession ("that was never true, I mis-tapped") and 康复 = a new event ("it was true, it is now resolved"). One clears the record, the other closes it. They should not share a screen.

5 · Your decisions, recorded

3 · Weight. Two variants during piglet processing (litter total, and individual once identity exists); individual capture once pigs are bigger. So the model is not a free toggle — it is determined by life stage and identity: no identity → group total only; identity → individual. Production's mode switch disappears.
4 · 移除 vs 康复. 移除 = a mis-entry correction; 康复 = clinical recovery. Different semantics, different history, and — per §4 — different mechanisms. Splitting them.
6 · Fostering. Piglets moved from their birth dam to another sow for production reasons (litter size vs functional teats, weak piglets needing a better dam, evening up litters). Confirms the Pairing archetype: one donor sow, one receiving sow, N piglets — so "bulk" in production's annotation means N piglets, not N sows. Question 6 closed.
7 · Equipment. Kept in the model as a second subject alongside pigs, so the UI consolidation reserves room for it: a device list scoped to pen/unit, a fault as a Record Sheet, and a resolution event. We do not design it now, but nothing we build should assume "the list is always pigs."
8 · Toolbox. Exists, out of scope. Record browsing lives there.

6 · The check-in — my recommendation

Your instinct is right that a heavy checklist across many units becomes hostile. The way out is to ask what a sign-off actually adds once events commit themselves.

Only assert what cannot be derived. Events prove a worker was in a unit and what he found. What they cannot prove is the null case — "I walked it and there was nothing to report." That is the only thing a sign-off adds, so that is all it should say.
ProposalDetail
One tap, no formCheck unit stamps unit · person · time. It records alongside what was found: inspected · 3 findings or inspected · nothing to report.
Available from the unit listSo a worker sweeping eight quiet units taps eight times without entering each — the "annoying at scale" failure mode disappears.
Checklist is per-farm configuration, default emptyFarms with audit requirements can add 2–5 items (water, feed, temperature, mortality removed, biosecurity). Empty config = one tap. Never more than one screen.
Environment + biosecurity readings become their own eventThey are measurements of a unit, not a certification of the pigs. Recording them is optional and independent — which is what production already does, minus the false claim that all pens and devices were checked.
No coverage assertionWe never claim "all pens checked" — production claims it and validates nothing. "Last checked" is a claim by a named person at a time, which is honest and audit-usable.

7 · Health triage — domain grounding

Production's four levels (紧急治疗 · 优先干预 · 常规观察 · 健康) are severity adjectives with placeholder descriptions. In commercial swine practice the triage decision a stockperson makes at the pen is not "how bad is this" but what do I do with her now — the widely-taught framing is treat / move / euthanize, and timely euthanasia of poor-prognosis animals is a welfare-audit item in most assurance schemes.

What production is missing. Two of the three real decisions have no level: move to a hospital pen (standard infrastructure — sick pigs are pulled from the group) and euthanize (the decision most sensitive to delay, and the one auditors examine). A scale that cannot express them will be worked around on paper.

Proposed levels — each names an action, so the level does something rather than colouring a row:

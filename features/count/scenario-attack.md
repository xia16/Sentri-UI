# Count / Transfer: an ordinary-day attack

Design review, not an implementation test. Read PRD → transfer flow → backend research → mocks/generator → earlier blind pass → house rules; opened all PNGs named in the brief, plus the condition and log mocks. Latest dated owner rulings take precedence, including destination-only recording and per-pen counting inside a unit recount. This does not reopen the blind pass's generic arguments about counting, missing identities, condition defaults, or offline ordering.

**Evidence:** screen copy in quotation marks is exact; proposed fixes are not existing copy. **Open** means the design gives no answer: the record outcome described is a risk, not a claim about implemented behaviour. Frequency and ranking are inferred from this simulated day, not farm observations. A **blocker** risks a wrong stock, identity, health, or closure record without a clear recovery; **confusing** invites a recoverable mistake or stalls work; **minor** costs attention without changing the record directly.

**Owner answers (2026-11-05):** P1–P3 whole-pen counts that replace (a count is a state, offline-safe); P4 accepted as is; P5/P19 a pig not on this move is taken where scanned, with an amber chip, never gated; P6 moving clears untagged conditions; P7 the map names its unit; P8 reopen the pen and count again; P13 Move toast blue; P22 dead on the truck = Not arrived, lost. See the PRD, *Owner rulings on the scenario attack*.

## 1. Top 10

Ranked by estimated frequency × harm. IDs follow this ranking; the timeline shows when they occur.

**1 — P1: Counting residents as arrivals.** Mei puts six arrivals into C2, which already holds nine. On `mock-13-receive-plan.png` she sees 9; the form in `mock-12-receive-pen.png` asks “How many pigs are in A3?” (C2 in this scenario). She correctly enters 15 and taps “Done”. The backend needs `no_identity_count` of arrivals: six. No screen separates the nine residents, so a literal submission books nine extra arrivals and removes nine too many from the source. **Open mapping.**

Smallest fix: show the retained residents and the derived arrival quantity before applying an occupied-pen count.

**2 — P2: A green pen is still filling.** At 07:31 Mei counts nine in A1; at 07:33 six more enter. `mock-14-receive-count.png` leaves the tile green under “Counted”. She either trusts it, leaving six arrivals unrecorded, or reopens and enters 15. `mock-12-receive-pen.png` does not say whether that replaces nine or receives another 15; correcting 15 to 14 is equally undefined. Count's later-observation rule alone does not define repeated `transfer_in` receipts. **Open.**

Smallest fix: make reopening a received pen replace that task's earlier tally, with the resulting arrival adjustment visible.

**3 — P3: Two correct counts become two receipts.** Mei and Hao each count the same 15 pigs; Hao's phone is offline. Both tap “Done” on `mock-12-receive-pen.png` and see their own green map. Each thinks the other can see the result. If both receipts apply, 15 physical pigs become 30 received; if one supersedes the other, neither worker sees whose observation stands. The PRD specifies competing Count observations, but not shared Transfer pen tallies. **Open.**

Smallest fix: share one task/pen tally and show when another worker's count replaces it, including a pending upload.

**4 — P4: Ending one crew's work ends everyone's.** Mei opens `mock-16b-end-early.png` while Unit 10 is still unloading and Hao holds an offline receipt. “Ready to end early” and “Goes on the balancing sheet” make holding “End task early” look like a valid end to her shift. It closes the whole task: live deliveries become unresolved shortages. The effect of a later upload or receipt is unspecified; `mock-16c-end-ready.png` also has no pending-work qualification. **Open closure boundary.**

Smallest fix: show outstanding work by destination and pending receipts at closure; require their resolution or explicit handoff before the global hold.

**5 — P5: The Stay pig actually arrives.** Bo accidentally loads 000901 instead of 000904. Mei scans her in `mock-12-receive-pen.png` and taps “Done”, or assigns her through `mock-14s-receive-scan.png`. Nothing drawn carries the source's “Stay · 000901” verdict into receiving. Accepting her silently violates the plan; rejecting her without a placement path leaves a real pig unrecorded. Equal head counts cannot explain the substitution. **Open admission rule.**

Smallest fix: flag a non-task arrival and require an explicit return or authorised task correction before treating her as received.

**6 — P6: Mixed arrivals have no defensible source debit.** Eleven anonymous pigs from A3 mix with four from B2 in destination B1. Mei can establish 15 in `mock-12-receive-pen.png`, but cannot tell which source supplied which sick pig. The receiving page has only a batch footer; `mock-7-noid-conditions.png` asks “With Coughing” for a single source elsewhere in the proposal. Neither the source reduction nor the condition split is specified across this many-to-one arrival. A guessed allocation corrupts source stock or health placement. **Open.**

Smallest fix: retain source-group and condition allocation with each receipt, and expose any allocation that remains unresolved.

**7 — P7: The right-looking map is the wrong unit.** The source's `mock-9-transfer-out.png` says “120 pigs to Unit 9”, while ending includes Unit 10. Both receiving maps (`mock-13-receive-plan.png`, `mock-14-receive-count.png`) say “Receive pigs” and “Batch 25 · from Farrowing room 3”, without naming the destination. Hao returns from Unit 10 to an identical pen code and taps B2; the phone can place pigs in the wrong unit without a contradictory label. The missing destination and source/end disagreement are definite; the overview used to switch units is undrawn.

Smallest fix: name the destination on the map and show the same per-unit allocation at the source and overview.

**8 — P8: Three sows scanned, three different crates.** Jin walks past sows in B2, B3 and B4. `mock-14s-receive-scan.png` says “3 scanned · tap their pen” and offers only “Clear”. She taps B2 expecting to choose the first sow; `mock-14t-receive-placed.png` answers “3 pigs put in B2”. All three identities now occupy B2, potentially a single-sow crate. The design has no drawn way to split that buffer or reverse a mistaken placement.

Smallest fix: let Jin select an individual buffered sow for placement and undo the last assignment.

**9 — P9: Found late, but only tagged pigs are actionable.** After opening `mock-16d-not-arrived.png`, Mei finds two anonymous piglets behind a gate. “13 pigs” / “Without ID” is a plain number; only ticking an ID produces “Move to” in `mock-16e-not-arrived-move.png`. She can back out and recount a destination pen before closing, but the page does not guide her there, and no post-closure path is specified. Two physically recovered pigs may remain losses or be added through a disconnected recount. **Post-closure outcome open.**

Smallest fix: give the anonymous number a quantity-to-pen receipt path that also resolves the original shortage after closure.

**10 — P10: An unchanged total hides changed identities.** A week later, a recount finds 000512 from another unit but fails to read resident 000803; the total stays 59. `mock-3-count-pen.png` ends with “Done”. Under the latest ruling, “Finish recount” in `mock-10-unit-recount.png` saves immediately because the total is unchanged: the “Found from other units” and “Not found” review in `mock-11-unit-balance.png` never appears. A stray read and an unread tag can therefore cause a cross-unit move and a balancing-sheet absence without final review.

Smallest fix: open the existing balance review for identity or placement changes even when the net head count is zero.

## 4. Questions only the owner can answer

1. **What does a receiving pen tally mean when residents exist or pigs arrive in waves?** Recommend: whole-pen observation, with residents retained and only this task's arrivals booked; subsequent tallies replace the earlier task/pen tally and reconcile its source debit. A correction must not manufacture a reverse physical move. **P1–P3.**

2. **Who may close a multi-unit task while another crew is receiving, particularly offline, and what happens to late receipts?** Recommend: one designated closer; show each destination's handoff and known pending work. Closure may leave genuine unresolved pigs, as already ruled, but must not silently discard a later valid receipt or convert it into a second arrival. Unknown offline work requires an explicit crew handoff, not a claim that the app knows every phone's state. **P3–P4.**

3. **May a receiver accept a Stay pig or a pig outside the selected batch/task?** Recommend: record the physical exception, then require an authorised selection correction or return; never count a substitute as the intended identity. Keep head count and identity completion separate. **P5, P17, P19.**

4. **Which anonymous source groups shrink, and which health counts move, after mixed receiving?** Recommend: explicitly retain source contributions; no arbitrary FIFO/proportional debit presented as observed fact. If the crew cannot establish a condition split, retain that uncertainty for follow-up instead of asserting zero or copying cases. This is a missing handoff in the destination-only flow, not a request to revive source counting. **P6.**

5. **Where are selected pigs before receiving, and what exactly happens to each kind of “Not arrived” at early end?** Recommend: selection is intent, not proof of departure. Distinguish confirmed still at source, reported dead, and unresolved location; keep a named pig's last known location traceable without asserting physical presence. Counts during the task must not book the same absence independently. Define anonymous recovery and wrong-unit recovery as resolutions of the original shortage. **P9, P14, P22, P27.**

6. **Does receiving an already placed sow mean reassignment, and may an occupied crate receive another sow?** Recommend: reassignment moves her once, adds zero arrivals, and updates both placements; an existing recorded occupant must be explicitly resolved, never silently evicted. **P8, P20, P23.**

7. **Can a partial recount apply unit-wide gains/losses while other pens retain records, and can it skip review when identities change?** Recommend: retain skipped pens as ruled, but call out retained versus observed stock; do not present a partial-unit discrepancy as proof of new/lost pigs. Review identity changes even at zero net. This preserves partial work without claiming an entire unit was physically reconciled. **P10, P26.**

8. **Are capacity and sick-pen placement advisory or prohibitions?** Recommend: accept a truthful observed count above capacity with acknowledgement, then leave it visibly actionable; never cap the number to make the plan fit. Clearly identify sick pens. **P15–P16.**

9. **What must a supervisor be able to reconstruct?** Recommend: task selection, actual receiving observations by person/time/unit/pen, replacements, early-end dispositions, and subsequent resolutions. Never describe unobserved loading as confirmed departure. The data model has operators and times; the phone still needs a route to this evidence. **P13, P24–P25.**
